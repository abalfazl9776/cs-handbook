**Tell Me About Yourself?**
Normally you shouldn't repeat the bullet points on your resume. This is an opportunity to stand out among other candidates, to present something you couldn't in your resume. You can start by asking should I elaborate more on what I said on my resume and clarification on them (cause the might not have seen it thoroughly) or you need anything else (maybe more interesting about how you do your current tasks, what you're great at, your exact current responsibilities, your past, the way you've come, and your future plans). DON'T BE FUNNY OR SAY ANYTHING PERSONNAL. They are not giving the interviewer any reason that they should hire you or not.

**How are you?**
The interviewer is already taking a mental note. They are asking them selves "Is this how they will show up on a hard day at work?". So YOU ARE ALWAYS GREAT.

**Why do you want to work here?**
It's not about **YOU**. It's about **THEM**. They don't care what you hope to achieve by working at their company. They want to know what problems you can solve for them based on your experience and knowledge, and what different approaches you would take to tackle those problems.

**Where do you see yourself in five years?**
It's not about YOU, again. They don't care about your plans and ambitions. They just want to know if you are leaving in six month or not. And also, if you're goals align with their in long term. So you should say something that the job and getting expert in that field and solving some problems already known.

**Why are you looking for a new opportunity?**
THEY DON'T WANT TO KNOW ABOUT YOUR PROBLEMS WITH YOUR CURRENT BOSS. Because in that exact moment you become a risk for them. They think "what are they going to say about us in 2 years?". Just say I've learned a lot in my current role and I'm looking for new challenges (but don't focus on challenges too much, cause they may think as soon as there won't be a challenge you will say your goodbyes).

**How do you feel about your current manger?**
Be careful about this one, because the person who is interviewing you may be your next boss. So say nothing NEGATIVE about your current boss, leadership or coworkers. Just say you've learned a lot from him, you respect him and ....

**What are your hobbies?**
You're painting a picture of who you are when you are not at work, so if you answer this with common hobbies that every body say, like watching TV, that makes you forgettable. Say something interesting and different so they may think "Uh, I want to get coffee with that person!". Reading, riding, learning a new language hiking and ... . Say something that makes the impression that you have life outside of work.

**About your previous job title and description**
Own your job, if you're leading the project but you have title of a mid level developer, claim the title. Cause your current title is what HR decided. You can say, "while my title was X i was doing A and B.". This is so important. Don't underestimate yourself.

**Do you have any questions for us?**
Have at least two questions ready. Cause if you say no, they'll think you're trying to find a way to end the conversation and also it communicates this message: "You're not that interested, you haven't thought beyond today, and you're just trying to get through this.".
The questions you askes make this impression that you already picture yourself in this role, doing so helps that the interviewer also consider you more suitable for the role that the others. Some questions can be found [[Questions to ask at the end of the Interview]]. Don't bring up anything about compensation and payments. That will be discussed in another session. Unless they bring it up themselves.
