**Less follow-ups is better**
When you are answering a question, try to present the important parts of the story at the right time. Providing the right context at the right time, so that the listener does not miss important information, is just as important as providing the context itself.
This is because one of the things the interviewer is assessing is your ability to communicate your ideas clearly and efficiently (as this is a core part of what you will do every day in the role), without unnecessary friction, back-and-forth, or requiring the interviewer to ask follow-up questions to understand what you mean.
Be proactive in telling details that interviewer might be interested in or if your not sure about that, ask the interviewer, don't let him ask the follow-up.

**Everything is about your thought process**
When interviewers ask questions like, “Tell me about a calculated risk you took,” they want to understand whether you recognized the significance of your decision, considered its potential consequences, and understood its impact on the business.
To help them assess this, you need to provide enough context for them to understand your reasoning and evaluate your decision-making process. **It’s not just about what you did; it’s about why you made that decision, what you considered before making it, and how you assessed the potential risks and trade-offs.**
